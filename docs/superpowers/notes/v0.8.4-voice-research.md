# v0.8.4 W7 research (D17): a cloned voice for Dom, and a Singaporean accent

Question (owner, 2026-11-25, "yep" to D17): is Seed Audio (or similar) voice cloning an option for Dom's voice, and could Dom have a Singaporean-accented voice trained from the IMDA National Speech Corpus? Research only; nothing was built or run.

**How this was researched.** Official pages were fetched and read (ByteDance Seed, OpenRouter, IMDA, data.gov.sg, arXiv, GitHub). Each claim cites its URL. A claim that only third-party write-ups made is marked *(secondary)*. No audio was generated and no key was used, so cost and retention figures are the listed ones, not measured. Page dates were read on 2026-11-26.

## Short answer

| | Verdict |
|---|---|

## (a) Seed Audio and voice cloning

**What it is.** ByteDance Seed's non-streaming audio generation model. It "lets creators shape a voice from a text description, an authorized reference sample, or both — without training a separate model for each speaker", across 20+ languages including English and Chinese. It is "now available through BytePlus" (Seed blog: <https://seed.bytedance.com/en/blog/from-speech-to-audio-creation-introducing-the-seed-audio-1-0-audio-creation-model>). The same page uses "authorized" for the reference sample, so a clone needs the speaker's permission.

**API availability.**

- **OpenRouter.** `bytedance-seed/seed-audio-1-0`, `POST /api/v1/audio/speech`, "optionally guided by a Seed speaker ID or a reference audio clip for voice cloning", output capped at 120 s a request, **$0.15 per minute**, listed released 25 Sep 2026, one provider ("Seed") (<https://openrouter.ai/bytedance-seed/seed-audio-1-0>). The sibling `bytedance-seed/seed-tts-3-0` is plain streaming TTS at $20 per million characters, with fixed voice names and no cloning in its listing (<https://openrouter.ai/bytedance-seed/seed-tts-3-0>).
- **Direct.** BytePlus (the Seed blog links its console). Third-party hosts also list the model *(secondary)*. No direct route is needed: OpenRouter's is the same shape as Dom's existing voice backups.
- **Reference clip limits.** Not on the OpenRouter page. Third-party pages say up to three clips of about 30 s *(secondary, and one source says 5–10 s is enough)*. Settle by testing.

**Fit with Dom.** A new voice backup would be an `openrouter_tts` route with a `voice` or reference field. Today's backup `voice` setting (added in W7) takes only a name, so cloning would need a reference-audio setting stored in the home, not in `config.toml`. A 10 s note costs about $0.025 at the listed rate. It would go through `studio_voice` usage and the spend cap unchanged.

**Licensing.** Seed Audio is a hosted service; its terms are BytePlus's and OpenRouter's. No model licence applies because there are no weights. What matters is the reference sample: the "authorized" wording means the owner must have the right to clone whoever is in it. Cloning a real person who has not agreed is a hard no, and cloning the owner's own voice is theirs to choose. I did not find a BytePlus page spelling out commercial terms or consent checks (secondary sources say commercial cloning can need a manual verification step; unconfirmed).

**ZDR and retention.** Not stated on either OpenRouter listing. Dom's media key is ZDR by default (see the OpenRouter notes in the repo) and a backup that cannot honour that must be marked `no_training` or `any`, with the owner's say-so. **Test with a real call before adding it**: send a `provider.zdr = true` request and see whether OpenRouter refuses it. Until then treat Seed Audio as non-ZDR.

**Local on Apple Silicon.** No. ByteDance says of the earlier Seed-TTS: "we will NOT be releasing the source code and model weights" (<https://github.com/BytedanceSpeech/seed-tts-eval>). I found no weights release for Seed Audio either; its only listed access is API. Open models with cloning that do run on a Mac are in (b).

**Quality.** ByteDance's own A/B tests show preference gains in generating voices from text descriptions (same blog); I found no independent listening test of its cloning. Judge by ear on three of the owner's reference lines before committing.

**Recommendation.** If the owner wants a distinct, owned voice: try Seed Audio through a new backup in a one-off test (about $0.15 for a minute of samples), with the owner's own voice or a text-described voice, and decide on retention after the probe. Do not make it the first choice for private replies: a non-ZDR route sees Dom's spoken text, which is why the existing privacy rules keep media text screened.

## (b) A Singaporean-accented voice from the IMDA National Speech Corpus

**The corpus.** The National Speech Corpus (NSC) is IMDA's Singapore English corpus: the first release has "more than 2000 hours of orthographically transcribed read speech", with a further ~1000 hours of conversational speech planned, "available for public and commercial use upon request ... under the Singapore Open Data License" (Interspeech 2019 paper: <https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2019/koh19_interspeech.html>). IMDA's 2018 factsheet says the same of release 1.0: 2,000 hours of locally accented audio with transcripts, "made available via the Singapore Open Data Licence" (<https://www.imda.gov.sg/-/media/imda/files/sg-digital/annex-g---no-embargo-factsheet-for-ai-open-source-libraries.pdf>). The download page is <https://www.imda.gov.sg/about-imda/emerging-technologies-and-research/artificial-intelligence/national-speech-corpus>; its FAQ is script-rendered and I could not read it, so any request-form conditions are unconfirmed. IMDA gives a contact address for questions on that page. Ask before training.

**Licence terms** (Singapore Open Data Licence v1.0, <https://data.gov.sg/open-data-licence>):

- A "worldwide, perpetual, royalty-free, non-exclusive" licence to "use, access, download, copy, distribute, transmit, modify and adapt the datasets, or any derived analyses or applications, whether commercially or non-commercially". Training or fine-tuning a TTS model is a derived application: **allowed, including commercial use**.
- **Attribution is required**, in the form `[Contains information from {dataset} accessed on {date} from {source} which is made available under the terms of the Singapore Open Data Licence version 1.0 {URL}]`. For a Telegram bot, that notice belongs in Dom's docs and the model card of the fine-tune, not in each voice note.
- Dataset provided "as is"; no warranty. The licence does not clear personal data or rights of the people recorded; see risks.

**Has it been done?** Yes. "Singlish, Can or Not? Fine-Tuning and Evaluating Zero-Shot TTS for Singapore English" (Kukanov and Chai, SLT 2026, arXiv v2 12 Oct 2026) fine-tuned Chatterbox and CosyVoice 3 on 50 NSC speakers and found fine-tuning "raises accent similarity on in-domain and out-of-domain speakers for both", with the gain persisting for held-out speakers (<https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23027>). Off-the-shelf zero-shot systems "reproduce a speaker's timbre while flattening the accent toward generic English", which is what an accent-by-reference-clip approach would get.

**What could be fine-tuned on the studio Mac.**

| Model | Licence | Mac story | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chatterbox (Resemble AI) | MIT; README says it is "retired research" since Aug 2026 and adds an imperceptible watermark to every output | Runs on `mps`; the paper trained only the autoregressive token module and kept the decoder and encoder frozen | Proven for this exact task; unmaintained is a risk (<https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox>) |
| CosyVoice 3 | Apache 2.0 (<https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice>) | Mostly CUDA tooling; Mac training unproven here | Proven in the same paper |
| Qwen3-TTS 1.7B, Orpheus 3B, OuteTTS 1B, Spark-TTS 0.5B, Sesame CSM 1B | each model's own (check before use) | mlx-tune does LoRA fine-tuning of these on Apple Silicon, recommending 16 GB+ unified memory (<https://arahim3.github.io/mlx-tune/audio.html>) | Easiest local path; no published Singlish result for them |
| Higgs Audio (today's voice) | Boson research, non-commercial (see `docs/studio.md`) | Runs in the studio now | Fine-tuning not documented; the licence already limits it |

**Effort.** Roughly, in order: request and download NSC (a 50-speaker slice of the read speech is all the paper used); pick 50 to 200 speakers with the clearest audio and one accent target; resample, trim and filter by transcript; LoRA fine-tune (mlx-tune route) or adapt the paper's Chatterbox recipe; listen-test with the owner; wire the result in as a local worker beside the others in `studio_service`. My estimate, not measured: two to three working days for a first usable voice if the Mac route works, a week with listening rounds. Check the studio Mac's unified memory against mlx-tune's 16 GB+ guidance before choosing a 3B base; training speed, not fit, is the usual limit.

**Risks.**

1. **Speaker likeness and consent.** NSC speakers were recorded for speech recognition; the licence is silent on voice-likeness uses. A model trained on a few speakers can reproduce them. Mitigate: train on many speakers (50 or more, mixed), never prompt with a corpus clip, and do not market the voice as anyone's. Ask IMDA in the same message as the download request.
2. **Accent caricature.** "Singlish" is a register as well as an accent. A model trained on read speech will do the accent in careful sentences and miss the particles and rhythm of chat. The conversational release (planned in the Interspeech paper) fits Dom's chatty voice better; check whether it is available.
3. **Attribution and reuse.** Attribution is required, and the data's own terms travel with any dataset or model card Dom publishes. The repo is public: do not commit audio, transcripts or weights.
4. **Quality regression.** Fine-tuning can degrade naturalness or stability; the paper measures naturalness and intelligibility for that reason. Keep the current voice as the fallback.
5. **Maintenance.** An unmaintained base model (Chatterbox) means no upstream fixes. Prefer a base with a live community when the choice is otherwise even.

**Recommendation.** If the owner wants it: contact IMDA for the NSC download and the voice-use question, then spike the mlx-tune LoRA path on the studio Mac with 50 speakers and a Qwen3-TTS or Orpheus base, judged against the Chatterbox result in the paper. Do not start before the owner has listened to the current Higgs voice and Seed Audio samples: a distinct voice might come from (a) alone.
